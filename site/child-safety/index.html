Child safety standards

Whirl for Android · last updated 21 October 2026

Whirl is an unofficial reader for the Whirlpool forums (whirlpool.net.au), written by Dallin Howes and published as free software. This page sets out Whirl's standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), as Google Play requires of apps in its Social category.

Our standard

Whirl has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation, including child sexual abuse material (CSAM), grooming, and any content or behaviour that sexualises or endangers children. Such content must never be posted on Whirlpool, shared through links opened from Whirl, or used in any way with the app.

How Whirl works, and what that means for content

Whirl does not host, store on any server, or distribute content of its own. It has no servers and no user accounts, and you cannot post from the app. It displays public pages and API data from Whirlpool, using your own Whirlpool API key, and keeps a short-lived copy on your phone for offline reading. Everything shown in Whirl was posted on Whirlpool, where Whirlpool's own rules and moderators apply.

Reporting from the app

  • Report a post: long-press any post (or, with TalkBack, use its actions) and choose Report to Whirlpool moderators. This opens Whirlpool's own report form for that post, where you sign in to Whirlpool. Whirlpool's form is for recent posts; for an older post it directs you to its Talk with a mod forum, which reaches the same moderators.
  • Report a concern to the developer: in Whirl, open the menu, then About → Report a concern, or email whirl@howesthat.net.au.

What we do with a report

When we become aware of child sexual abuse material or child exploitation shown in Whirl, we:

  • alert Whirlpool's moderators so the content is removed at its source;
  • report it to the Australian Centre to Counter Child Exploitation (accce.gov.au/report) and the eSafety Commissioner (esafety.gov.au/report), and to other authorities where the law requires;
  • where needed, stop Whirl from showing that content in an update of the app; and
  • cooperate with law enforcement and comply with Australian child safety laws.

If a child is in danger

If a child is in immediate danger, call 000 in Australia, or your local emergency number. You can report child sexual abuse material directly to the ACCCE or eSafety in Australia, or to the NCMEC CyberTipline (report.cybertip.org) from elsewhere.
