Petdevils writes...

And what do people mean by GREY importers

Here's my understanding.

It means that the seller isn't "officially authorised to sell the product". It isn't illegal – just isn't officially recognised by the manufacturer. For example a grey importer will buy them cheap from a place like Hong Kong and then re-sell them to Australians at a lower-than-retail price.

This usually means you're missing out on a quality guarantee that you come to expect when buying from an Australian retailer.

For a smartphone it might mean

  • Warranty differences (such as 1 year instead of 2 year) or perhaps the warranty covers less than normal
  • OS differences such as getting an operating system designed for a Chinese carrier
  • Potentially getting bloatware or malware (unlikely but I've heard stories)
  • Claiming warranty might take a long time – 4-6 weeks or more compared to 1-2 weeks
  • Grey importers might make more of a fuss and charge you for shipping when claiming warranty compared to an authorised Australian retailer that will make the process simple and convenient (I claimed warranty with JB Hi Fi for an iPhone 5S and the process was simple, quick and didn't cost me anything – I was given a brand-new phone because they couldn't repair it).
  • Probably no "price guarantees* if the price drops shortly after you bought it

Basically you're getting a cheaper phone but missing out on some conveniences and assurances that come from buying from an authorised Australian retailer.

I bought my Nokia 1520 for about $450 AUD from Kogan in Jul 2014. Pretty good price for a flagship device with those specs. I knew the risks of getting a grey import (it's a Hong Kong device) but I've been pretty lucky and I'm still using it to this day.

My recommendation is that if you have a few spare phes and is prepare to wait for repairs, then you can go for a grey import. Usually after a year and you have no issues, you have saved some money.

If you have only one phone, you should buy an A-tick phone.
