Paul Warren writes...
["What you need to be doing though, is eating more of the right foods.

You just need to eat the right foods at the right time."]

I totally agree. I've never had an issue with weight, I actually struggle to put weight on, and can eat whatever crap I want, but I choose not to, because it just makes me feel bloated and tired.

I've grown up always eating healthy food, we hardly ever got takeaway, and I'm thankful for that. I do agree though that cooking for one person is very difficult, seems such a lot of work just for one person.

The trick to it is to cook in bulk. Set one day/night aside to cook several dishes up. Buy 7-10 or so plastic containers (decor or tupperware, good solid ones not disposable cheap ones, and ones that are microwave safe), and then just "serve" up what you cook into the containers, one for each night of the week. If you cook about 3 dishes, then you can have a different one each night. You are effectively then doing the same as Lite N Easy, by just pulling one out of the freezer each night and microwaving.

Make sure you eat lots of fruit and vegies and reduce your meat intake. Vegies are not as fattening (if at all). Steamed potatoes, carrots, brocolli, cauliflower, beans, etc is very good for you, it will make you feel full, but not put on weight.

If you are thinking of roasting vegies, then use Olive Oil (Extra Virgin), much more healthy than Vegetable or other oils, in fact use Olive Oil for all your cooking, it is expensive, but it's so much better. Also, use it sparingly.

Secondly, if you feel a bit peckish during the day, eat some fruit. Nibble on carrot/celery sticks. Eat an Apple, etc. Don't reach for lollies/muesli bars, etc.

The main thing I think is to cut out a lot of sugary/fatty foods, and possibly limit food intake slightly (if you eat alot), but not just to limit food in general.

Lastly, as others have mentioned, drink plenty of water – 2-3L+ per day. It can be hard to drink it at first, but your body will get used to it. I often add some lemon juice to the water to make it easier to drink. Cut out soft drinks, etc.
Also, when you start drinking increased water, you'll pee a lot at first, it feels like every 10 minutes, but after a few weeks your body gets used to it and you won't pee as frequently.

OP, good luck with it, I really hope you stick with it and succeed!!

Personally.. I love carbs.. and I do get hungry and want to snack. The difference I guess is that I tend to make my own snacks. Healthier and I can control the portions. I also eat and chew slowly so my tummy has a chance to tell me when it's full. I don't think there is an issue with snacking as long as you snack on the right foods.

Some thoughts:
- Swap out soft drink for soda water (with a little lemon or honey if you really need it). I used to be addicted to Coke until I realized it was the fizziness I loved. Hot tea is also great for making you full..
- If you feel hungry, drink some water or tea, then wait 5 minutes to see if you are still hungry.. often we mistake hunger for thirst. I keep a bottle of water at my desk and refill it about 5 or 6 times through the day... still feel hungry but it's enough to stop me running downstairs for chocolate.
- Get a potato chip maker (like http://www.tokyomango.com/tokyo_mango/2007/02/microwave_potat.html). This is the best thing I have ever brought.. you can have chips without any oil and they taste as good as the full fat version, if not better as they are piping hot ;) I also do pasrnsip/sweet potato chips too.
- Popcorn is another filling snack. You can get unpopped kernels from coles and just nuke half a handful in a brown paper bag twisted closed. A $1 bag will last you months compared to the ready made buttery fat versions.

Some really great suggestions on here, esp miss wb!

Ultimately, if you are interested in weight loss, and maintaining it in the long run, it should be a diet that you are comfortable with.

Crash diets, work-outs are great short term, but long term, you'll find that you slip back into your old ways again.

Drink lots of water (about 2 litres a day).
Here's something i read somewhere: A lot of us do not drink enough (and that includes me), and our bodies are often 'hungry' for water as well as food. Before a meal, drink 1-2 glasses a water. By the time you are done with your meal, you should feel satisfied.

Make your own food! As cliche as it sounds, breakfast has to be the biggest meal, followed by lunch and dinner. Cut carbs out from at least one meal of the day.

Vegetables and Salads..... make them a feature of your meals.

Snack in between meals if you hungry. I'm not so sure about this one, but i've heard that it helps your metabolic rate.

Good luck with these!
